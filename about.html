Scientific Methodology & Architecture

Engineered for Himalayan Slope Resilience

SlopeGuard provides high-resolution terrain analysis, precipitation tracking, and geotechnical monitoring across vulnerable sectors in the North Eastern Region (NER) of India.

How SlopeGuard Operates

Three interconnected stages converting raw geological telemetry into life-saving early warning notices.

01

Continuous Data Ingestion

Aggregates automated rain gauge networks (ARG), piezometer pore pressure sensors, and European Space Agency Sentinel-1 InSAR synthetic aperture radar passes over steep mountain corridors.

02

AI-Based Risk Assessment

Computational limit-equilibrium stability models combine real-time soil saturation thresholds with high-resolution digital elevation models (DEM) to estimate factor-of-safety reduction.

03

Early Warning & Alert Fan-Out

When threshold breaches are confirmed or reviewed by geotechnical officers, push advisories and SMS bulletins are dispatched to district disaster management authorities and registered residents.

Information Classification Types

SlopeGuard clearly distinguishes algorithmic telemetry from human-reviewed advisories.

AI ESTIMATE

Algorithmic Telemetry

Continuous automated readings from rain gauges and saturation sensors. Rapidly indicates changing slope equilibrium before manual survey crews can be dispatched.

REVIEWED ALERT

Staff-Reviewed Notice

Telemetry breaches verified by geotechnical monitoring staff or district disaster officers, checking sensor validity against Doppler radar and ground deformation trends.

OFFICIAL ADVISORY

Government Bulletin

Formal bulletins published in coordination with District Disaster Management Authorities (DDMA), Geological Survey of India (GSI), and State Emergency Operations Centers.

Primary Data Sources

  • India Meteorological Department (IMD): 24h precipitation records and gridded monsoon forecasts.
  • Geological Survey of India (GSI): Historical landslide inventory and susceptibility zonation.
  • Sentinel-1 InSAR: Interferometric synthetic aperture radar surface displacement measurements.
  • Field Sensor Arrays: Localized borehole piezometers and telemetry tiltmeters installed across high-risk corridors.

Known Operational Limitations

  • Unmonitored Sectors: Geographic areas outside instrumented sectors are not assessed. The absence of an alert marker does not indicate that an unmonitored mountain slope is safe.
  • Satellite Pass Latency: InSAR surface deformation tracks depend on 6 to 12-day orbital revisit cycles.
  • Extreme Cloudbursts: Localized micro-burst precipitation exceeding 100mm/hr can induce debris flows faster than mathematical telemetry polling cycles.

Frequently Asked Questions

Operational clarity on alerts, offline caching, and government coordination.

Does SlopeGuard replace district evacuation orders?
No. SlopeGuard is a technical early warning platform designed to assist decision-makers and citizens. In any emergency, residents must prioritize official instructions and evacuation orders issued by their District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA) or the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF).
Can I access monitoring data when cellular networks fail during storms?
Yes. SlopeGuard includes an offline-first service worker and IndexedDB cache. When you lose cellular connection, previously synchronized risk classifications, emergency numbers, and sector coordinates remain accessible, with explicit timestamps showing when the data was last received.
How are risk levels (Critical, High, Moderate, Low) computed?
Risk levels are determined by multi-factor safety equations factoring in 24-hour and 72-hour antecedent rainfall indices, slope gradient steepness, soil pore water saturation, and geological fault proximity.
